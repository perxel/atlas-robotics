Nobody schedules the moments that actually define an office's culture. They happen in the twenty minutes between meetings, in a break room, over a game nobody officially sanctioned. This is what one ordinary week of ours actually looked like.

## The Jenga tower nobody would knock down

It started as a five-minute distraction and survived three full days on the break-room table, growing more structurally questionable by the hour. By Wednesday it had its own group chat thread. Nobody wanted to be the one who took it down — turns out shared low-stakes tension is a surprisingly good team-bonding mechanism.

## The meeting that wandered somewhere better

A planning meeting scheduled for thirty minutes ran for ninety, not because it went badly but because the conversation kept turning up better questions than the ones on the agenda. We've stopped treating that as a failure of time management — some of our best decisions have come from meetings that refused to stay on script.

## Coffee breaks are load-bearing

The forty-minute coffee-break debate that reshaped part of a roadmap wasn't an accident of a slow morning — informal conversation is where people say the blunt version of an opinion they'd soften in a status meeting. We've started treating the break room less like downtime and more like an actual part of how decisions get made here.

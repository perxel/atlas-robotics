Most "productivity tool" roundups measure the wrong thing — they measure what people downloaded, not what they still use a season later. Here's what actually survived past the first-week novelty on our team.

## The rule that mattered more than any app

The single biggest change wasn't software: it was a team-wide agreement to keep one visible task per screen during deep-work blocks, no background chat tab, no half-open inbox. It sounds almost too simple to write down, and it's also the habit with the most obvious before-and-after in how much actually gets shipped in a focused morning.

## Deep-work blocks, protected on purpose

We schedule two-hour focus blocks on the team calendar, same as any other meeting — which means people stop treating "no meetings" time as automatically available for anyone who wants it. Protecting the time on the calendar did more than any app-level focus mode ever did.

## What we dropped

We tried, and dropped, more time-tracking and gamified-focus apps than we'd like to admit. The pattern was consistent: anything that added a second thing to think about while trying to focus lost out to anything that just removed a distraction and got out of the way.

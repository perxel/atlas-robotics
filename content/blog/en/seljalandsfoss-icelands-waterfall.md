There's a reason Seljalandsfoss shows up in nearly every Iceland itinerary, and it isn't just the 60-meter drop. It's the path: a narrow, often slippery loop that lets you walk in a full circle around the falls, including the stretch directly behind the curtain of water.

## The walk behind the water

Standing behind Seljalandsfoss changes the whole experience. From the front it's a waterfall. From behind, with the afternoon sun coming through the spray, it's closer to standing inside weather — the roar is different, the light is different, and the ground underfoot is genuinely unstable in places.

## Pack for wet, not just cold

The single most common mistake here isn't underestimating the temperature — it's underestimating the spray radius. Bring a real rain shell, not a fashion jacket, and expect your camera lens to need wiping every thirty seconds if you're shooting from the ledge.

## Go at golden hour if you can

Seljalandsfoss faces roughly southwest, which means late-day light comes through the water instead of bouncing off it — the difference between a flat photo and one where the whole falls seems lit from inside.

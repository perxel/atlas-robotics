If you asked five people on your team what eats the most time in a normal week, at least three of them would describe some version of the same thing: waiting on an approval, chasing a handoff, or updating a tracker by hand after the fact.

None of that work is hard. It's just repetitive, and repetitive work is exactly what automation is good at.

## 1. Approval requests

Anything that follows "someone submits it, someone else signs off" is a candidate. Set the rule once — who approves what, and what happens next — and stop routing requests manually.

## 2. New-hire onboarding checklists

Account creation, equipment requests, welcome emails: the same seven steps, every time, for every new hire. A checklist in a doc relies on someone remembering to open it.

## 3. Status handoffs between teams

Support escalates to engineering, engineering hands back to support once it's fixed — this is usually a Slack message that's easy to lose in a busy channel. A trigger-based handoff can't get buried.

## 4. Weekly reporting

If a report is "export this, copy that into a slide," it's a rule waiting to be written, not a task waiting to be remembered.

## 5. Renewal and follow-up reminders

The stuff that only matters on a specific date is the easiest thing to forget when you're busy — and the easiest thing to automate, since a calendar doesn't get busy.

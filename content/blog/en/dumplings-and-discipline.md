Somewhere around the hundredth dumpling of the weekend, the folding stopped being something we thought about and started being something our hands just did. That's the entire point of a task this repetitive, and it's harder to get from a single recipe than from doing the boring version two hundred times over.

## The first twenty are the hardest

The first batch of dumplings looked exactly like first-batch dumplings always look — uneven pleats, wrappers torn at the edges, filling escaping mid-fold. Nobody's hands know the motion yet, and no amount of watching a video first changes that; it only comes from doing it badly enough times to stop doing it badly.

## What actually mattered wasn't the fold

We went in assuming the fold pattern was the skill to master. It wasn't — the wrapper thickness and the steam timing mattered far more to the final result than whether the pleats were even. A slightly ugly dumpling with the right wrapper thickness beats a perfectly pleated one with a wrapper rolled too thick, every time.

## The boring part is the actual lesson

By the last batch, the fold had gone from a conscious decision to something closer to muscle memory — which is really what a weekend like this is for. Not a better dumpling on day one, but hands that know the motion by day two.

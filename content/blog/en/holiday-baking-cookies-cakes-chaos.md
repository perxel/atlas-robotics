Every family or team has one holiday baking day a year that starts organized and ends in flour on the ceiling. This is ours, told without editing out the parts that went wrong.

## The plan versus the afternoon

The plan was three kinds of cookies and one cake, done in sequence, oven scheduled like a small production line. The afternoon had other ideas: two ovens, four people, and dough that kept getting re-rolled past the point it should have been, because nobody wanted to be the one who threw scraps away.

## The cake that almost didn't make it

The cake's frosting refused to set for the better part of an hour, right up until someone remembered the fridge existed. It went out slightly under-chilled and got eaten within fifteen minutes anyway — which is either a lesson about perfectionism or just a fact about how holiday cake actually gets judged.

## What we're doing differently next year

One oven, one tray at a time, no exceptions — the overlap was the source of nearly every problem. Everything else about the chaos, we're keeping exactly as it is.

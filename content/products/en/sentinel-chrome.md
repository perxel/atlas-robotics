Sentinel Chrome isn't trying to disappear into the background — it's built for the opposite job. Museums, flagship stores, and research showrooms install it precisely because people stop and look.

## Movement that reads as natural

34 degrees of freedom let Sentinel Chrome gesture, turn, and shift its weight the way a person would, instead of the stiff pan-and-tilt motion most service robots default to. It's a deliberate design choice: a robot that moves naturally gets approached, not avoided.

## Runs the room without the cloud

Speech recognition, face tracking, and gesture response all run on-device, so there's no lag waiting on a network round-trip and no video feed leaving the building — a requirement more than one retail client asked for outright.

Shells are swappable in under ten minutes, so the same unit can wear a brand's finish for a launch event and a neutral chrome shell the rest of the year.
